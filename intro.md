You might recognize this image — it’s the album cover of Unknown Pleasures by Joy Division. It’s been referenced everywhere in pop culture, from T-shirts to posters. But what’s really interesting is that it actually came from scientific data.

It was originally a visualization made by a radio astronomer named Harold Craft back in 1970. Each line you see represents radio signals emitted by a pulsar — basically a rapidly spinning neutron star out in space. Later, the graphic designer Peter Saville used it for the album cover, and it became this cultural icon. And at the same time, people began calling similar charts “Joyplots” in the data-viz community.

I chose it because to me, it captures what data visualization is all about — this blend between art and science. It’s a reminder that data isn’t just rows of number, it has an artistic aspect - it can be expressive, emotional, and beautiful.

# About This Class

This course explores how data can be transformed into expressive and meaningful visual (and sonic) experiences. You will learn to analyze, design, and build data-driven works that combine clarity, creativity, and technical skill. Through weekly demos, hands-on projects, and discussions, you’ll gain both a strong conceptual foundation and the freedom to explore and express data.

- **Schedule**: Mondays, 6:30–9:30 PM · 11/3-12/15
- **Prerequisites**: None (basic JS helpful)
- **Grading**: P/F
- **Reading**: All reading will be provided, no purchase required
- **Applications**: Observable, and Illustrator or Figma (optional)
